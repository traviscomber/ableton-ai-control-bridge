# Night Protocol 001 — Production Brief

Status: **READY FOR SONGPLAN**  
State: **Night / Dark Techno**  
Music owner: **Venom**  
Technical execution: **Ultron**  
Strategic gate: **Doom**

## Core intent

Create the first definitive DARKSCO track: dark, physical, cinematic, industrial, and disciplined. It must feel like a controlled subterranean system coming online rather than a collection of aggressive techno conventions.

The track should establish DARKSCO's Night language through pressure, scale, repetition, negative space, and precise transformation. Darkness must come from structure and atmosphere, not uncontrolled distortion or noise.

## Listener effect

The listener should move through four psychological states:

1. **Detection** — distant machinery and low-frequency signals appear.
2. **Alignment** — the groove locks into a precise hypnotic system.
3. **Activation** — the track reaches physical and cinematic scale.
4. **Aftermath** — energy withdraws, leaving the system present but unresolved.

## Technical frame

- Tempo: **136 BPM**
- Time signature: **4/4**
- Target duration: **6:00–6:40**
- Arrangement target: **192–224 bars**
- Primary key centre: **F minor or non-functional F tonal centre**
- Headroom target before mastering: **at least -6 dBFS peak on the stereo bus**
- No master limiter during arrangement review

## Track architecture

Use stable `track_ref` identifiers in the SongPlan and command sequence.

| Track ref | Role | Function |
|---|---|---|
| `night_kick` | Kick | Controlled low-end anchor; stable, not over-distorted |
| `night_sub` | Sub/bass | Monophonic pressure and restrained movement |
| `night_perc_main` | Main percussion | Industrial pulse and groove definition |
| `night_perc_detail` | Detail percussion | Microvariation, metallic transients, spatial events |
| `night_motif` | Tonal motif | Minimal memorable identity; no conventional lead melody |
| `night_atmos` | Atmosphere | Subterranean scale, distant machinery, air and depth |
| `night_impact` | Impacts/transitions | Section punctuation and controlled energy changes |
| `night_texture` | Resampled texture | Organic-machine signature and evolving instability |

The first automated version may use MIDI placeholders and stock-device-compatible structures. Sound replacement and resampling can follow after arrangement proof.

## Rhythmic language

### Kick

- Four-on-the-floor foundation.
- Consistent transient and low-frequency body.
- Avoid excessive rumble that removes rhythmic definition.
- Introduce short kick omissions only at structural transitions.

### Bass

- Short, controlled sub events with deliberate space around the kick.
- Use one primary cell and two bounded variations.
- Avoid continuous rolling bass that obscures the track's scale.

### Percussion

- Main groove should feel mechanical but not static.
- Use microvariation every 4 or 8 bars.
- Metallic details should imply a large physical environment.
- Avoid generic open-hat-on-every-offbeat dependence.

## Tonal identity

Create a motif that can be recognized after one listen but remains minimal:

- Two to four pitch classes.
- Short rhythmic cell.
- Restricted register.
- Introduced as a signal rather than a melody.
- Variation through filtering, octave displacement, timing, and spatial treatment.

Do not imitate any recognizable artist, track, label, or signature patch.

## Arrangement

### A. Detection — bars 1–32

- Atmosphere and low-level machine texture.
- Introduce a filtered or partial kick signal after the opening space.
- Establish one recurring sonic symbol.
- Keep energy below full groove.

### B. Alignment — bars 33–80

- Full kick and primary bass cell enter.
- Add main percussion in layers.
- Introduce the motif indirectly.
- Build confidence through small changes rather than large effects.

### C. Pressure — bars 81–128

- Increase density and stereo activity without losing low-end control.
- Develop the motif into its clearest form.
- Add one transition that changes perceived scale.
- Maintain hypnotic continuity.

### D. Void event — bars 129–144

- Major negative-space event.
- Remove or radically reduce the kick for at least 8 bars.
- Preserve tension with atmosphere, sub signal, or motif fragment.
- Avoid a conventional festival-style breakdown.

### E. Activation — bars 145–192

- Strongest physical section.
- Return the groove with a meaningful variation, not a simple copy.
- Use the widest controlled spatial image here.
- Add one unexpected but coherent event between bars 168–184.

### F. Aftermath — bars 193–224

- Remove elements in a deliberate hierarchy.
- Leave one unresolved machine or tonal signal.
- End with enough DJ usability for mixing, but preserve narrative identity.

## Automation and variation rules

- Every 8-bar block must contain at least one intentional change.
- No parameter should move continuously without an audible structural reason.
- Macro automation should use bounded values and be recoverable.
- Repetition is allowed only when it increases hypnosis or scale.
- Avoid copy-paste sections longer than 16 bars without microvariation.

## Sound-design direction

Preferred characteristics:

- Deep spatial environments
- Controlled low-frequency pressure
- Metallic and mineral transients
- Distant industrial resonance
- Restrained distortion
- Organic-machine textures
- Long tails used selectively
- Silence and negative space as active elements

Avoid:

- Generic cyberpunk effects
- Horror stingers
- Excessive risers and impacts
- Uncontrolled saturation
- Supersaw leads
- EDM breakdown/drop grammar
- Unlicensed vocal or cinematic samples
- Presets used without transformation when they define the track's identity

## Stock-device-first policy

The first executable SongPlan should prefer Ableton Live 11 stock instruments and effects or neutral MIDI placeholders. Any third-party device requirement must be explicitly listed before execution.

The initial automated session should prove:

- Track creation and naming
- Scene and clip structure
- MIDI note placement
- Mixer state
- Clip naming and colors
- Basic macro or parameter control where a known rack exists
- ACK-complete execution

It does not need to produce the final sound solely through automation.

## Review criteria

Venom will assess:

- Originality
- Groove authority
- Low-end discipline
- Structural progression
- Negative-space effectiveness
- Motif memorability
- DARKSCO identity
- Mix potential
- Repeat-listening value

A complete arrangement is **not ready** when it sounds generic, overfilled, sonically uncontrolled, or dependent on familiar dark-techno clichés.

10. Expected Ableton state after completion.
