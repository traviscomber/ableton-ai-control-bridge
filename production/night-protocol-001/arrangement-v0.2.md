## Objective

Turn the generated six-scene skeleton into a coherent 224-bar Arrangement View performance with authored transitions, bounded microvariation, one signature event, and enough space for deliberate sound design.

This document does not claim the track is mixed or release-ready. It defines the next Ableton production pass.

## Arrangement map

| Bars | Section | Required development |
|---|---|---|
| 1–32 | Detection | Atmosphere, machine texture, partial kick signal, first motif fragment |
| 33–80 | Alignment | Full groove enters in layers; bass and percussion establish authority |
| 81–128 | Pressure | Density, motif clarity, stereo movement, controlled escalation |
| 129–144 | Void Event | Kick removed for at least 8 bars; tension remains through signal and atmosphere |
| 145–192 | Activation | Strongest groove variation, widest image, signature event at bars 168–184 |
| 193–224 | Aftermath | Elements removed in hierarchy; unresolved signal remains for DJ-compatible exit |

## Transition design

### Detection → Alignment — bars 25–40

Preparation:

- Bars 25–28: expose a filtered kick transient every two beats.
- Bars 29–32: shorten atmosphere tails and narrow stereo width.
- Bar 32 beat 4: remove all transient material for one beat.

Event:

- Bar 33: full kick and first sub cell enter together.
- Main percussion enters after four bars, not on the same downbeat.

Consequence:

- Bars 37–40: introduce the motif indirectly through filtering or octave restriction.

### Alignment → Pressure — bars 73–88

Preparation:

- Add one metallic detail variation at bar 73.
- Reduce bass activity during bars 77–80.
- Increase atmosphere width gradually, bounded to avoid phase instability.

Event:

- Bar 81: restore bass with Cell B and add one new percussion layer.

Consequence:

- Bars 81–88: reveal the motif in its clearest form while retaining groove continuity.

### Pressure → Void — bars 121–136

Preparation:

- Bars 121–124: remove one percussion layer.
- Bars 125–128: simplify bass and extend the final motif note.
- Bar 128 beat 4: capture a short reverb or delay tail.

Event:

- Bar 129: kick stops.
- Keep only atmosphere, isolated signal, sub residue, and one sparse metallic event.

Consequence:

- Bars 129–136: reduce density further rather than rebuilding immediately.

### Void → Activation — bars 137–152

Preparation:

- Bars 137–140: introduce filtered sub pulses without the kick.
- Bars 141–144: narrow the complete mix toward mono and reduce high-frequency detail.

Event:

- Bar 145: return kick and bass with the Activation variations.
- Restore stereo width over two bars rather than instantly.

Consequence:

- Bars 149–152: add percussion detail and the activated motif in stages.

### Activation → Aftermath — bars 185–200

Preparation:

- Bars 185–188: remove the highest percussion details.
- Bars 189–192: reduce bass variation and narrow the motif register.

Event:

- Bar 193: remove impact and main texture layers.

Consequence:

- Bars 193–208: reduce elements in this order: detail percussion, bass variation, main percussion, kick density.
- Bars 209–224: retain atmosphere, unresolved motif signal, and sparse DJ-usable kick or texture.

## Signature event — bars 168–184

The signature event must be recognizable without becoming an EDM breakdown.

1. Bars 168–171: narrow the mix toward mono and remove detail percussion.
2. Bars 172–175: distort or resample the machine texture while the groove remains stable.
3. Bar 176: one-bar kick omission; sub and motif continue.
4. Bars 177–180: motif returns one octave lower or with a shifted rhythmic placement.
5. Bars 181–184: restore full width and the strongest groove variation.

Use one clear sonic transformation. Do not stack unrelated risers, impacts, and fills.

## Microvariation rules

- Every 4 bars: change velocity, omit one hit, or move one detail event.
- Every 8 bars: alter one percussion or bass cell.
- Every 16 bars: add, remove, or transform one structural layer.
- No 16-bar block may be an exact duplicate of the previous block.
- The kick remains stable; variation should mostly come from omissions at transitions.
- Bass variations must leave physical space for the kick.
- Motif variations are limited to octave, timing, filtering, note omission, and spatial treatment.

## Sound assignment pass

Use Ableton Live 11 stock devices or clearly documented sources.

| Track ref | Initial production direction |
|---|---|
| `night_kick` | Short, deep kick; controlled decay; no excessive rumble |
| `night_sub` | Mono sub; short notes; sidechain or envelope separation from kick |
| `night_perc_main` | Clean industrial pulse with restrained saturation |
| `night_perc_detail` | Sparse metallic events placed in a large environment |
| `night_motif` | Minimal transformed signal, not a conventional lead |
| `night_atmos` | Subterranean depth with automated width and filtered movement |
| `night_impact` | Structural punctuation only; maximum one major impact per section |
| `night_texture` | Resampled organic-machine material used as identity layer |

## Mix guardrails

- No limiter on the master during arrangement review.
- Maintain at least 6 dB peak headroom before mastering.
- Keep sub and kick mono below the chosen crossover region.
- Check the signature event and Void Event in mono.
- Long reverb tails must be edited or automated around kick re-entry.
- Loudness is not a quality gate during this pass.

## Exit gate

The v0.2 production pass is complete when:

- All five major transitions are implemented.
- The signature event is audible and coherent.
- Every 16-bar block contains meaningful development.
- All eight tracks have deliberate sound assignments.
- Peak headroom is at least 6 dB.
- A review WAV has been exported.
- Sample and device provenance has been recorded.
- Venom performs a listening review.
