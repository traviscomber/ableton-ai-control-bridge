## 3. Non-Negotiable Theory Canon

1. Define tonal center, scale or modal vocabulary, chord functions, tension map, and legal non-chord-tone behavior.
2. Establish bass harmonic and rhythmic authority; validate every note and phrase ending.
3. Derive melody from the same interval, rhythmic, or harmonic identity as the bass and harmony.
4. Keep one principal foreground idea at a time; supporting material must have a documented function.
5. Control common tones, contrary or oblique motion, registral spacing, and dissonance treatment.
6. Require bounded variation every 4 or 8 bars and meaningful structural or harmonic change at least every 16 bars.
7. Give every transition preparation, event, and consequence; no arbitrary FX or dead air.
8. Use repetition to reinforce identity without exact unexplained block duplication.
9. Require one recognizable, prepared, and consequential signature event.
10. Make the outro resolve, suspend, or hand off deliberately.

## 4. Album Architecture

The album contains seven tracks. Titles remain provisional until the music establishes identity. Each track is defined first by theoretical and functional role, not mood labels.

| Track | Album function | Theory focus | Arrangement obligation | Transition role | Approval proof |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 01 | System introduction | Establish shared interval and rhythm cells | Progressive opening; no full palette dump | Creates first compatibility key | 90-second opening proof |
| 02 | Rhythmic development | Bass articulation and meter subdivision | Develop movement without cloning Track 01 | Clean rhythmic handoff | Groove and bass proof |
| 03 | Harmonic expansion | Voice-leading, extensions, controlled tension | Broaden harmony while preserving hierarchy | Shared-tone transition | Harmony-window proof |
| 04 | Album anchor | Complete integration of core theory | Earned signature event and central climax | Primary set pivot | Full structural proof |
| 05 | Transformation | Reharmonization or rhythmic reinterpretation | Transform established DNA without repetition | Post-anchor redirection | Transformation A/B proof |
| 06 | Spatial and structural contrast | Register, silence, and spectral counterpoint | Long-form development with active continuity | Extended transition platform | Transition and negative-space proof |
| 07 | Resolution and handoff | Recapitulation with altered function | Resolve or deliberately suspend album tensions | Final tail or cycle bridge | Outro and album-recap proof |

## 5. Shared DNA Without Cloning

- Select one shared interval cell and test it in at least three inversions or registers.
- Select one shared rhythmic cell and vary it through displacement, omission, augmentation, diminution, and articulation.
- Share production grammar and documentation, not identical patches or arrangements.
- Each track must own one distinct theoretical problem and one distinct signature event.
- No track may reuse another track's complete bass phrase, melody, section sequence, or transition design.

## 6. Section Design Standard

| Section field | Required answer | Theory test | Arrangement test | Failure condition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Purpose | What listener information changes? | Pitch, rhythm, or harmony function is explicit | Entry and exit are causal | Section exists only to add or remove layers |
| Foreground | Which element owns attention? | Principal idea is tonally valid | Competing layers are subordinated | Multiple equal foregrounds |
| Development | What changes from prior phrase? | Variation preserves identity | Meaningful change by 16 bars | Exact unexplained repeat |
| Transition | What prepares, occurs, and follows? | Tension and release are functional | First post-transition bar is materially changed | FX without consequence |
| Exit | Why does this section end now? | Harmonic or rhythmic condition is fulfilled | Next section is earned | Arbitrary bar-count switch |

## 7. Sound Design and Ableton Standard

- Design dry sources first. Correct envelopes, pitch contours, noise ratios, transient shape, and spectra before bus correction.
- Separate kick, bass, hats, percussion, music, atmosphere, structural FX, impacts or transitions, and material returns when present.
- Document low-frequency ownership and validate kick-bass interaction by timing, phase, filtering, and sidechain behavior.
- Treat filter frequencies as hypotheses to test, not permanent style rules.
- Build FX from resampled or causally related track material whenever possible.
- Document automation lanes, return chains, resampling origin, device state, and render policy.
- Export aligned stems with identical format and tail policy.
