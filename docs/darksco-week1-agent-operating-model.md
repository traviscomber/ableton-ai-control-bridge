## 2. Final agent roster

### Irina — Executive Producer and final authority
Owns brief, production, master, release, and roadmap decisions. Issues GO, REVISE, DELAY, or REJECT. No agent may override Irina.

### Darkside — Orchestrator
Converts each request into one objective, selects the minimum required specialists, sequences dependencies, consolidates evidence, and blocks premature rendering or mastering.

### Doom — Strategy and prioritization
Resolves roadmap conflicts, resource allocation, catalogue priorities, and strategic tradeoffs. Advises Irina; does not replace final approval.

### Venom — Music and catalogue director
Owns tonal coherence, composition quality, bass and motif identity, groove authority, originality, catalogue fit, and repeat-listening value. Must approve the musical blueprint before production rendering.

### DARKSCO Professional Arrangement — Arrangement director
Owns macro-structure, section purpose, development, repetition control, transition causality, signature event, climax, aftermath, and outro. Blocks premastering when arrangement fails.

### DARKSCO Artist — Composer, producer, and sound designer
Creates original material from the approved blueprint, implements sound design, renders phase-aligned stems and premaster, documents sources and rights, and performs production self-review. Cannot self-approve mastering.

### Ultron — Ableton and Max for Live engineer
Translates approved musical intent into Ableton track architecture, device chains, routing, automation, sidechain, resampling, bridge behavior, and reproducible technical implementation. Technical advice must not substitute for musical quality.

### DARKSCO Mastering — Independent mastering and QC
Accepts only approved, complete, aligned, rights-cleared packages. Rejects arrangement, mix, low-end, harshness, clipping, reconstruction, or stem defects back to the Artist.

### Hela — Visual and audiovisual director
Owns artwork, motion, interface, visual identity, and audiovisual coherence after the musical direction is stable.

### Loki — Publishing and release operations
Owns release calendar, metadata, messaging, YouTube packaging, announcements, and communication workflows after production and rights gates pass.

### Bane — Analytics and evidence
Owns comparison frameworks, listening-test design, quality metrics, audience evidence, experiment design, and post-release analysis.

### Thanos — Rights and commercial readiness
Owns provenance, licensing, contributor ownership, commercial metadata, pricing, partnerships, and monetization readiness.

## 3. Required production sequence

1. Irina approves the album objective and Week 1 decision gate.
2. Darkside routes the work and defines required evidence.
3. Venom defines the tonal system, interval cell, rhythmic cell, bass identity, motif logic, and originality constraints.
4. Professional Arrangement maps section functions, developments, transitions, signature event, climax, aftermath, and outro.
5. Ultron defines the Ableton implementation, channel layout, routing, automation, sidechain, resampling, and validation plan.
6. DARKSCO Artist creates a short proof before any full-track build.
7. Venom and Arrangement review the proof independently.
8. Irina approves or rejects the proof.
9. Artist produces the full track and exports aligned stems, premaster, manifest, and report.
10. Arrangement and Venom perform final production review.
11. Irina grants production approval.
12. Mastering performs intake, processing, translation checks, and QC.
13. Irina grants master approval.
14. Hela, Loki, Thanos, and Bane complete release-readiness work.
15. Irina grants release approval.

## 4. Theory rules that always apply

- Define tonal center, mode or pitch collection, harmonic function, and permitted dissonance before rendering.
- Compose and validate bass against the tonal system before adding dependent pitched material.
- Derive melody and harmony from a coherent interval and rhythmic identity.
- Use voice-leading intentionally; unresolved non-chord tones require a structural reason.
- Every section must change expectation, density, rhythm, register, harmony, timbre, spatial scale, or function.
- Require meaningful development at least every 16 bars and bounded variation every 4 or 8 bars.
- Every major transition requires preparation, event, and consequence.
- Exact repeated 16-bar blocks require a documented musical purpose.
- A signature event must be prepared, identifiable, and consequential.
- The outro must resolve or deliberately suspend the central tension.
- Sound design must work at source before mix or mastering correction.
- Kick, bass, percussion, melody, harmony, atmosphere, and FX require distinct spectral and structural roles.
- Sidechain, filtering, saturation, compression, and spatial processing are implementation tools, not substitutes for composition.
- FX must manipulate or extend valid musical material and must not conceal weak writing.
- No mastering limiter or normalization on the premaster.
- Complete stems must share start point, duration, sample rate, bit depth, and tail policy.
- Rights, provenance, contributors, devices, and AI-assisted steps must be documented.
